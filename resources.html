Outside resources, by topic

Big O

  • Khan Academy: Big-O notation - the single best first read. Upper bounds, dropping constants, and why O(log n) shows up in binary search. Written with Thomas Cormen (co-author of CLRS).
  • Khan Academy: Asymptotic notation (unit) - the theory behind the lesson rules.
  • Khan Academy: Recursive algorithms (unit) - the recursion lesson source: factorial, palindromes, and the properties of recursive algorithms.
  • GeeksforGeeks: Space complexity and auxiliary space - auxiliary space as the extra space beyond the input, with recursion-stack examples.
  • bigocheatsheet.com - lookup table for data structure operation costs.
  • Princeton COS 126: Performance lecture notes (PDF) - order of growth with a worked two-sum example.
  • UW CSE 332: Asymptotic analysis (PDF) - the formal definition of Big Oh plus recurrence relations.
  • Educative: Big-O notation for coding interviews and beyond - a structured, math-light alternative when a lesson does not click.

Python foundations

  • The Python Tutorial - the official tutorial: data structures, slicing, sorting, and identifiers, straight from the source.
  • The Python Language Reference - the exact rules: operator precedence, identifier grammar, and the reserved keyword list.

ADTs and linear structures

  • algs4: Bags, Queues, and Stacks - the canonical university treatment of ADTs, data types defined by their API.
  • GeeksforGeeks: Abstract Data Types - a short exam-level definition of what versus how.

Stacks, queues, deques, and priority queues

  • algs4: Bags, Queues, and Stacks - the canonical university treatment of the LIFO and FIFO contracts.
  • algs4: Priority Queues - the canonical treatment of highest-priority removal.

Linked lists

  • algs4: Bags, Queues, and Stacks - the canonical university treatment of linked structures and why insertion and removal are reference updates.
  • Wikipedia: Linked list - the variant vocabulary: singly, doubly, and circular, side by side.

Hash tables

  • algs4: Hash Tables - the canonical university treatment of hash functions, collisions, and load factor.
  • Python tutorial: Dictionaries - the official treatment of the exam’s code: store, retrieve, and lookup by key.

Sorting and selection

  • algs4: Quicksort - the canonical treatment of pivoting and partitioning.
  • Wikipedia: Counting sort - the counts array and the O(N + K) analysis.

Huffman coding

  • CLRS: Introduction to Algorithms (MIT Press) - the canonical text; Chapter 16.3, Huffman Codes, derives the shorter-codes-for-frequent-characters rule.
  • Wikipedia: Huffman coding - the reference overview of lossless compression and the variable-length code idea.

Exam strategy

  • Wikipedia: Spacing effect - the evidence behind spacing out retrieval practice instead of cramming.
