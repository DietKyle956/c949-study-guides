Stacks and queues: LIFO vs FIFO

The exam gives you the principle - “last in, first out” or “first in, first out” - and asks you to name the structure, its operations, or the right end of it. This lesson maps the principles to their answers, then drills the recognition until it is instant.

Why this lesson

This is the same move as the ADTs course and the Big O course’s exam-day procedure: spot the defining property, then fire its answer. Two of the exam’s rows are the principles themselves - LIFO and FIFO. One more row is a tiny computation: given a queue’s front and length, name its back. All three become free points once the map is automatic.

The stack: one open end

A stack follows LIFO: last in, first out. It has a single working end, the top, and its three operations are:

OperationWhat it does
push(x)add x on top of the stack
pop()remove and return the top item
top()read the top item without removing it

The defining property: one open end

Everything enters and leaves through the top. The item pushed most recently is the one popped first - that is the whole of LIFO. Think of a stack of cafeteria trays: you take from the top of the pile, never from the bottom.

You have already met this menu in the ADTs course - push, pop, and top are the operations the exam lists for a stack. What is new here is the behavior: the exam asks which principle a stack follows, and the answer is the phrase itself, last in, first out.

The queue: two ends, two jobs

A queue follows FIFO: first in, first out. It has two working ends and its three operations are:

OperationWhat it does
enqueue(x)add x at the back
dequeue()remove and return the front item
front()read the front item without removing it

The defining property: two ends, two jobs

Items enter at the back and leave at the front. The item that has waited longest - the first one in - is the first one out. Think of a line: you join at the back, you are served at the front, and the first arrival is the first served.

The exam’s favorite distractor is swapping the ends: a queue does not add at the front or remove from the back. Spot “add at the back” and “remove from the front” and the answer is queue.

The exam’s queue computation

The one calculation the exam asks:

array       = [72, 15, 2, 14]
front_index = 0
length      = 4

All four elements are active. The front is 72 - the element at front_index. The back is the last active element, not the first:

Back = last active element

Count forward from the front. The front sits at index 0 and there are four active elements, so the active run is indices 0 through 3. The back is index 3: 14. In general the back is at index (front_index + length - 1) - here 0 + 4 - 1 = 3.

The trap is answering 72 again because “front” appears in the question. The front and the back are opposite ends: front 72, back 14.
